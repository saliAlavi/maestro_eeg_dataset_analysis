\section{Experimental setup}
\label{sec:setup}

Every result is defined by four choices: the candidate set, the split, the
decision window and the set of streams. Every accuracy comes with its own
shuffled baseline; an accuracy without one is not a result. Besides accuracy,
the baseline and the contribution, we report a permutation $p$-value
$(\#\{v_{\text{null}}\!\ge\!v\}+1)/(n+1)$, whose smallest possible value with
twenty shuffles is $0.048$; the Shapley credit $\phi_m$; the \emph{flip rate},
the fraction of windows whose chosen candidate changes when the stream is
shuffled; accuracy with the stream replaced by zeros; and the \emph{collapse},
the mean cosine similarity between the time-centred embeddings of different
windows, where a value near one means every window produces the same pattern.

Training uses AdamW \citep{loshchilov2019adamw} with learning rate $10^{-3}$,
batches of $32$ windows from one listener, and at most $50$ epochs. Checkpoints
are chosen on validation data by the rule of Section~\ref{sec:selection}, with
three shuffles, and the chosen checkpoint is scored once on the test set with
twenty. \textbf{We did no hyper-parameter search}: every setting was fixed once
in advance (Appendix~\ref{app:hp}), so these numbers are not tuned. To compare
architectures, the first row of our ablation runs the benchmark's model
\citep{maestro2026} --- its encoder, its cosine-then-linear score and plain
cross-entropy --- inside our training loop and splits, so that differences
between rows come from the model and not from the protocol. We run it on both
candidate sets. Neither run is meant to reproduce the benchmark's published
numbers, which use its own training loop, a longer schedule and all streams;
when we cite those numbers we take them from the paper.

\paragraph{Why this dataset.}
Crowding out between streams can only be studied when the streams are recorded
\emph{and} the listener is free to produce them. This is rare. Public AAD datasets
record EEG plus a few electrodes near the eyes for cleaning artifacts, and
nothing else \citep{fuglsang2018dtu}, and their designs deliberately hold the
listener still: earphones that simulate the room, a chin rest
\citep{yan2024fourspeaker}, a sound booth. The closest dataset, AV-GC-AAD
\citep{geirnaert2024avgc}, separates gaze from attention on purpose, to catch
decoders that read gaze. That is the right tool for asking \emph{whether} a
decoder uses gaze instead of the brain, and the wrong tool for asking how credit
is shared among five streams, because four of them have been designed to carry no
information. As far as we know, MAESTRO is the only public AAD dataset that
records eye tracking, head motion and scene video together with EEG from a
listener who is free to move in a room with several loudspeakers
(Appendix~\ref{app:corpora}). The parts of \merit{} that do not need these streams
--- the audio probe and the coupling score --- work on any matching dataset, and
we test them on two (Section~\ref{sec:res-mm}).
