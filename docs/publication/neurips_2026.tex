\begin{abstract}
We release MAESTRO, a multimodal auditory-attention-decoding (AAD) corpus in which 16 normal-hearing adults completed 100 cued-attention trials each while listening to four concurrent spatialised talkers at $\pm 22.5^\circ$ and $\pm 67.5^\circ$ plus back-hemisphere noise at $\pm 135^\circ$. Synchronously recorded streams: 32-channel \SI{500}{\Hz} EEG, binocular gaze and pupillometry, head-mounted scene video, head IMU, the per-trial stimulus audio for each device, and a comprehension-question response. Existing AAD corpora deliver EEG and stimulus envelopes only; MAESTRO is the first AAD release that ships gaze, IMU, and head-mounted scene video on the same trials, enabling the ``is this really an auditory-cortex signal or just overt orientation?'' question to be answered quantitatively rather than rhetorically. Around the data we define three task framings (attended hemisphere, attended eccentricity, 4-class attended source) and an envelope-reconstruction track, all packaged in a versioned evaluation protocol with four splits (within-subject, leave-one-subject-out, motion-residualised, high-quality tier), six decision windows for continuous AAD, and three mandatory baselines. Headline findings on this protocol: (i)~classical envelope-reconstruction TRF/CCA is at the chance floor of the paradigm ($0.523$ pooled hemisphere with the best of four backbones); (ii)~a 368-feature classical-spectral EEG classifier reaches $0.716$ $[0.641, 0.792]$, while a gaze-only baseline reaches $0.770$ $[0.728, 0.811]$; (iii)~a strict trial-level motion-residualisation that orthogonalises EEG features against gaze$+$IMU$+$video collapses spectral-EEG hemisphere AAD to $0.521$ $[0.488, 0.552]$, exposing a confound-controlled floor that any future ``purely neural'' decoder must beat; (iv)~at the trial level, gaze-based AAD tracks comprehension success ($r{=}{+}0.54$) whereas envelope-tracking decoders \emph{anti}-track it (Wilcoxon $p{=}0.034$ on the split $\delta{+}\theta$ TRF), dissociating effortful acoustic tracking from delayed comprehension. The dataset, loaders, $60{+}$ reproducible baseline results, and Croissant metadata record are hosted at an anonymous URL during review.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Auditory attention decoding (AAD) is the problem of inferring, from non-invasive recordings of a listener, which of several concurrent talkers they are attending to. Public datasets have driven progress by releasing EEG plus the attended and unattended audio: KUL~AAD~\cite{das2019aad}, DTU~AAD~\cite{fuglsang2018zenodo}, and recent MEG-AAD releases cover most published decoders. These datasets capture speech-tracking EEG but \emph{not} the behavioural channels that generate the recurring reviewer concern that spatial AAD is entangled with overt orientation: pupil dilation follows arousal, gaze trajectories lateralise toward the attended source~\cite{golumbic2013}, and subtle head orientation tracks effortful listening~\cite{hendrikse2019}. Without synchronous gaze and head-motion streams it is not possible to ask, on the same trials that support the AAD claim, how much of the classifier is neural and how much is overt behaviour.

\paragraph{What this dataset adds.} For every trial we have (i)~32-ch \SI{500}{\Hz} EEG; (ii)~binocular gaze direction, pupillometry, and scene-projected gaze; (iii)~\SI{100}{\Hz} head-mounted IMU; (iv)~\SI{25}{\Hz} egocentric scene video; (v)~the stereo audio played through each of three speaker devices; and (vi)~a comprehension question answered after every trial. No previously released AAD corpus bundles all five non-audio streams.

\paragraph{What we do with it.} We treat the corpus not as a bag of recordings but as the substrate for a \emph{contract}: the set of tasks, splits, windows, and baselines a paper must report on if it claims an AAD result on this data. Around that contract we ship reproducible baselines on three task framings and a strict motion-residualisation control that gives a confound-controlled floor against which any ``purely neural'' decoder must compete. We use the multimodality to settle---quantitatively, on the same trials---two long-running questions: \emph{is the EEG decoder eye-tracking in disguise?} and \emph{do AAD decoders track comprehension or effort?}

\paragraph{Contributions.}
(i) A 16-subject, 1600-trial AAD corpus with five simultaneously recorded modalities, including two (head-mounted scene video and IMU) never previously released with AAD (Sec.~\ref{sec:dataset}).
(ii) A versioned evaluation protocol with three task framings, four splits, six decision windows, and three mandatory baselines, packaged as a one-call Python harness (Sec.~\ref{sec:protocol}).
(iii) Reproducible baseline benchmarks across envelope-reconstruction TRF/CCA, a 368-feature classical-spectral EEG classifier, gaze/IMU/scene-video unimodal decoders, early/late/calibrated multimodal fusion, SNR- and window-stratified sweeps, learning curves, and leave-one-subject-out transfer (Sec.~\ref{sec:benchmarks}).
(iv) Diagnostic controls that exploit the multimodality: an aggressive cleaning ablation (Sec.~\ref{sec:cleaning}), a symmetric cross-modal predictability map between EEG, gaze, IMU, video, and audio (Sec.~\ref{sec:xmodal}), a strict trial-level motion-residualisation that yields a confound-controlled floor (Sec.~\ref{sec:resid}), and a trial-level behaviour-$\times$-decoder analysis that dissociates effortful acoustic tracking from comprehension (Sec.~\ref{sec:behxdec}).
(v) Croissant metadata, a Hugging Face / OpenNeuro mirror, and per-experiment SLURM scripts that reproduce every number in the paper from the raw recordings.

\paragraph{Scope.} This is a dataset and benchmarks paper, not a new modelling paper. The shipped baselines are deliberately standard---linear TRF, CCA, $\ell_2$-logistic regression on classical features, LightGBM, and reliability-weighted late fusion---so the numbers in Sec.~\ref{sec:benchmarks} represent a faithful floor, not a tuned ceiling, for future work. Deep-learning decoders are implemented as drop-in slots behind a \texttt{RUN\_DEEP} flag but are not run in this submission (Sec.~\ref{sec:limits}).

\section{Related AAD datasets}
\label{sec:related}

Public AAD corpora (Table~\ref{tab:related}) cover $\leq 44$ subjects and ship EEG-plus-audio only; \cite{fuglsang2020} adds in-ear EEG and listening-effort labels. The community has raised gaze, pupil dilation, and subtle head orientation as confounds on every AAD generation \cite{golumbic2013, hendrikse2019, geirnaert2021, geirnaert2024}, and recent MEG-AAD releases still cover only a single non-neural channel. No previously released AAD corpus bundles synchronised EEG, binocular eye-tracking \emph{and} pupillometry, head-mounted IMU, \emph{and} egocentric scene video on the same trials. This is the gap MAESTRO addresses.

\section{Dataset}
\label{sec:dataset}

16 adult native English speakers (self-reported normal hearing) completed one session each. After five familiarisation trials the participant was cued, on each of 100 evaluation trials, to attend to one of four spatial talkers while three other competing streams played simultaneously for \SI{30}{\second}. After every trial the participant answered a multiple-choice comprehension question about the cued stream; per-trial correctness is released as an attention-compliance label (mean compliance $0.82$, range $[0.61, 0.92]$; full psychometric in Appendix~\ref{app:behav}). Demographic questionnaires were collected only in a voluntary opt-in follow-up (5/16 subjects, ages 18--30, mixed gender); to preserve participant anonymity during review we release only the aggregate range.

\paragraph{Spatial geometry.} Six speakers form a horizontal ring at a radius of \SI{4}{ft}. Device~1 carries the two left-hemisphere speech speakers at azimuth $-67.5^\circ$ (Device-1~L) and $-22.5^\circ$ (Device-1~R); Device~2 carries the right-hemisphere pair at $+22.5^\circ$ (Device-2~L) and $+67.5^\circ$ (Device-2~R); Device~3 plays back-hemisphere multi-talker noise at $\pm 135^\circ$. Each device plays one stereo file with its two channels driving its left and right speakers at different powers; per-channel power and attended-speaker SNR are reported in \texttt{audio\_stimuli\_data/trials.csv}. SNR varies from \SI{3}{\dB} to \SI{18}{\dB} (median \SI{12}{\dB}, IQR \SIrange{10}{14}{\dB}). The attended-speaker label is one of $\{1,2,3,4\}$.

\paragraph{Modalities recorded per trial.} 32-channel \SI{500}{\Hz} EEG (ANT Neuro \emph{eego mylab}, 10--20 montage); binocular gaze direction, pupillometry, and scene-projected gaze at \SI{50}{\Hz} (Tobii Pro Glasses~3); \SI{100}{\Hz} head-mounted IMU; \SI{25}{\Hz}, $1920{\times}1080$ egocentric scene video; the stereo audio played through each of three devices. Hardware models, channel montage, sampling rates, storage layout, and the cross-modal temporal-alignment procedure are detailed in Appendices~\ref{app:hardware}, \ref{app:layout}, and \ref{app:align}.

\section{Tasks and evaluation protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}

A multimodal release is only useful to the community if different papers' numbers on it are comparable. We commit to protocol~v1.0 (versioned; future revisions preserve the v1.0 split so no number ever becomes orphaned). Every published accuracy on this dataset must report the (task, split, window, subject-subset) tuple; our harness \texttt{scripts/evaluation\_protocol.py} enforces this by construction.

\subsection{Tasks}

We define three classification framings over the four-speaker label, plus a continuous envelope-reconstruction track. \textbf{All three classification framings are required} in any paper reporting AAD on this dataset.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{T1 attended hemisphere} (chance $0.5$): $\{1,2\}$ (left, $\le -22.5^\circ$) vs.\ $\{3,4\}$ (right, $\ge +22.5^\circ$). The classical binary AAD framing. Future spatial-AAD methods that report only this number are interpretable but cannot be compared to ones that exploit eccentricity or source identity.
  \item \textbf{T2 attended eccentricity} (chance $0.5$): $\{2,3\}$ (inner, $\pm 22.5^\circ$) vs.\ $\{1,4\}$ (outer, $\pm 67.5^\circ$). Tests whether the decoder picks up eccentricity rather than hemisphere; gaze-based decoders show a much smaller eccentricity gradient than hemisphere gradient, so T1$-$T2 is a useful diagnostic of where a decoder's signal lives.
  \item \textbf{T3 4-class attended source} (chance $0.25$). The full label. The hardest task and the one that most strongly distinguishes information that is genuinely about source identity from information that is merely about side or distance.
  \item \textbf{T4 envelope reconstruction} (continuous; metric $\rho_{\rm att}-\rho_{\rm una}$ and binary attendance accuracy at decision windows $\{1,2,4,8,16,30\}$\,s). The classical TRF/CCA target. We report it because it is the lingua franca of AAD methods, and we report its bit-rate (information transfer rate) at every window so that real-time methods can be benchmarked against the same throughput axis.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Splits}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{S1 within-subject 5-fold stratified CV} (seed 0). The headline number. Reported as the unweighted mean across the 16 per-subject accuracies with bootstrap 95\,\% CI (10000 resamples over subjects).
  \item \textbf{S2 leave-one-subject-out (LOSO)}. Generalisation across subjects. Quantifies the per-subject calibration premium any deployable method must close.
  \item \textbf{S3 motion-residualised within-subject} (Sec.~\ref{sec:resid}). The confound-controlled floor: a method claiming purely neural gains must move \emph{this} number, not (or not only) S1. The shipped harness performs the residualisation inside every CV fold so no train/test motion-information leaks.
  \item \textbf{S4 high-quality-tier within-subject}: subjects with comprehension $\ge 0.80$ \emph{and} Tobii gaze-validity $\ge 0.70$ (pre-registered criteria, not post-hoc accuracy). Reports an optimistic ceiling while making the inclusion rule transparent; 8/16 subjects pass at v1.0.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Windows, metrics, and mandatory baselines}

The canonical decision window for T1--T3 is the full 30-s trial; continuous-AAD submissions must additionally report $\{1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 30\}$\,s accuracy on T4 and the corresponding ITR. Primary metric is accuracy with bootstrap 95\,\% CI; secondary metrics are per-subject scatter and ITR. A methods paper comparing to MAESTRO must report the three following baselines (reproducible by one command each, Appendix~\ref{app:repro}):

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{B1 Gaze-only logistic regression (S1, T1).} Hemisphere target $0.770$. The first number any new EEG decoder must clear before claiming a multimodal contribution.
  \item \textbf{B2 EEG-spectral 368-D logistic regression (S1, T1).} Hemisphere target $0.716$. The non-deep ceiling.
  \item \textbf{B3 EEG-spectral on motion-residualised features (S3, T1).} Hemisphere target $0.521$. \emph{Any EEG-method gain over B3 is the paper's headline.}
\end{itemize}

Multimodal methods must additionally report a \emph{marginal-gain} number: S1 accuracy minus the better of (a)~gaze-only and (b)~EEG-spectral-only. Without this, a multimodal paper cannot tell whether its gain comes from fusion or from one dominant modality; our fusion baselines (Sec.~\ref{sec:fusion}) make the point that pooled fusion can hide per-subject gain, and the protocol forces future papers to surface that.

\section{Benchmarks}
\label{sec:benchmarks}

\subsection{Envelope reconstruction (TRF/CCA): the floor of this paradigm}
\label{sec:trf}

We fit ridge backward TRFs and CCA over EEG lags $[-200, +500]\,\text{ms}$ against the 28-band gammatone envelope of the attended audio, with four backbones (broadband Hilbert envelope 1--9\,Hz; split $\delta+\theta$; mel-28; CCA on mel-28). Ridge $\alpha$ is selected by inner 5-fold CV on $10^{-1}\ldots 10^{6}$ scored by $\rho_{\rm att}-\rho_{\rm una}$.

Linear envelope reconstruction sits at the chance floor of the paradigm. Short windows ($\le 4$\,s) stay at chance; accuracy climbs only to $0.539$ at 30\,s (window sweep in Appendix~\ref{app:window}). The shallow gradient is a designed property: each device plays a single stereo file whose two channels drive its two speakers at different powers, so the envelope shape is near-identical between the two sides. Envelope reconstruction therefore cannot pick between the two speakers of the same device and collapses to chance on T3 (mean $0.255$, chance $0.25$). \textbf{Implication}: classical envelope-AAD decoders are a reporting requirement (T4) on this dataset, not a competitive headline. The paradigm decouples spatial source identity from envelope content---a feature for studying spatial attention in the multimodal regime, but a limitation for porting two-talker AAD decoders unchanged.

\subsection{Classical spectral EEG classifier}
\label{sec:spectral}

We compute a 368-dimensional feature vector per trial on 1--40\,Hz preprocessed EEG at \SI{500}{\Hz}: log Welch band powers in $\delta/\theta/\alpha/\beta/\gamma$ per channel (160), within-channel band ratios (96), four region-wise alpha-lateralisation indices, Hjorth mobility/complexity per channel (64), Shannon spectral entropy per channel (32), region-wise $1/f$ aperiodic slope (4), alpha-band MSC and wPLI within-left, within-right, and between-hemisphere (6), and global field power mean and std (2). $\ell_2$-logistic regression with 5-fold stratified CV per subject.

The 368-feature linear classifier reaches $0.716$ pooled hemisphere AAD---$+21.8$\,pp over the best linear-TRF backbone. LightGBM under-performs, characteristic of $p{=}368$ vs.\ $n{\approx}100$ per subject; regularised linear is the right default at this dataset size. Nine of 16 subjects pass $0.70$ hemisphere; five pass $0.88$. \textbf{Why this matters for AAD methodology}: the dominant published AAD decoder family (envelope reconstruction) is the wrong question to ask on this paradigm; a $368$-D linear classifier on classical spectral and connectivity features is at $+21.8$\,pp over it. Future deep-EEG methods on this data should benchmark against B2 ($0.716$), not against the envelope-TRF floor.

\subsection{Unimodal behavioural baselines: gaze, IMU, scene video}
\label{sec:behav-base}

A 23-dimensional per-trial gaze$+$IMU summary (scene-projected mean/std, per-eye azimuth/elevation, pupil diameter, sample validity, saccade rate and median amplitude, 3-D gaze centroid, head rotation magnitude) feeds $\ell_2$-logistic regression.\ \textbf{Gaze alone reaches $0.770$ $[0.728, 0.811]$ pooled hemisphere accuracy}; 11/16 subjects exceed $0.70$. Eccentricity (T2) is weaker ($0.683$): gaze is strongly lateralising but only weakly eccentricity-encoding. T3 gaze-only reaches $0.547$ ($+30$\,pp over chance), and gaze LOSO on T3 transfers to $0.475$ $[0.405, 0.548]$, the strongest cross-subject number in the release.

A 35-feature IMU baseline (per-axis accel/gyro stats plus dominant motion frequencies and spectral-band ratios) pools at $0.607$ $[0.541, 0.670]$ on T1; a 15-feature scene-video baseline (motion energy, brightness, dense optical-flow stats on a $160{\times}90$ downsample) pools at $0.617$ $[0.558, 0.675]$. The two are nearly identical---as expected, a head-mounted scene camera in a quiet room is largely a derivative of head motion. On T2 both rise to $\approx 0.66$, beating the EEG spectral classifier ($0.649$) on that task: the eccentricity cue is mostly carried by head orientation. \textbf{This is why MAESTRO ships head-motion modalities}: without IMU and head-mounted video, a $0.61$--$0.66$ confound floor on these tasks would be invisible.

\subsection{Generalisation sweeps: SNR, window, learning curve, LOSO}
\label{sec:sweeps}

Four stress tests are first-class results and required entries for the protocol (Sec.~\ref{sec:protocol}); summary numbers below, full tables in Appendices~\ref{app:snr}--\ref{app:loso}.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{SNR stratification.} Envelope-CCA hemisphere accuracy rises monotonically from $0.464$ at $\le 6$\,dB to $0.525$ at $\ge 15$\,dB ($+6.1$\,pp). The spectral classifier peaks in the middle bins ($0.670$ at 7--10\,dB)---weakest at low SNR (poor neural SNR despite high listening effort) and at high SNR (where the task is nearly trivial and listeners may lapse). Gaze accuracy is nearly flat across SNR, consistent with gaze indexing the spatial-attention decision rather than acoustic SNR per se.
  \item \textbf{Window sweep.} Envelope CCA stays at chance for $\le 4$\,s and reaches $0.539$ at 30\,s; the spectral classifier is defined only at the 30-s trial window. Continuous-AAD methods must report all six windows.
  \item \textbf{Learning curves.} Gaze-only saturates by $n_{\rm train}{=}40$; the spectral classifier is still gaining between $n{=}60$ and $n{=}80$, implying additional accuracy is recoverable from longer sessions. Pooled early fusion stays below gaze-only at every $n$, confirming the Sec.~\ref{sec:fusion} finding.
  \item \textbf{LOSO (S2).} Envelope CCA transfers at $0.539$; gaze 23-D at $0.715$; the spectral classifier at $0.615$ (drops $10.1$\,pp vs.\ S1, quantifying the per-subject calibration premium); concatenated EEG$+$gaze at $0.696$. Gaze dominates cross-subject because between-subject EEG topography variation is much larger than between-subject gaze-behaviour variation.
\end{itemize}

\section{Diagnostics: what is the EEG decoder actually decoding?}
\label{sec:controls}

The point of a multimodal release is that every confound a reviewer can name has a synchronous ground-truth recording on the same trials. We use that to answer four questions any AAD methods paper using MAESTRO will face.

\subsection{Cleaning ablation: does the signal survive aggressive nuisance removal?}
\label{sec:cleaning}

We re-run the 368-feature classifier under five EEG preparations: \emph{raw} (Sec.~\ref{sec:spectral}); \emph{ica\_eog} (ICA with automatic EOG-component removal using Fp1/Fpz/Fp2 as proxies); \emph{regress\_gaze} (channel-wise ridge regression of 13 Tobii-gaze features out of each EEG channel); \emph{regress\_gaze\_imu} (same plus 8 IMU features); \emph{drop\_eog\_emg} (exclude Fp1/Fpz/Fp2 as EOG sources and T7/T8/F7/F8/FC5/FC6 as EMG sources, leaving 23 channels).

Every cleaning step retains $\ge 0.67$ hemisphere AAD; channel-wise gaze$+$IMU regression costs only $-0.7$\,pp ($0.716 \to 0.709$); the largest drop ($-3.9$\,pp) comes from removing 9 EOG-/EMG-prone channels entirely. The remaining 23 central/parietal/occipital channels are exactly where auditory cortex projects (Cz, Pz, CP1/2, C3/4, P3/4, POz, O1/Oz/O2). \textbf{So the signal is not in the EOG-prone channels and does not vanish under standard nuisance removal at the time-series level.} This is the answer to the simplest version of ``is this just eye-tracking?''---no.

EEG spectral content is \emph{strongly} linearly related to head-motion amplitude, gaze validity, pupil diameter, and scene-motion energy ($r \in [0.45, 0.56]$ for the top feature in each), and \emph{essentially unrelated} to per-trial audio summary statistics ($|r| \le 0.09$). This reads at first like a contradiction with the cleaning ablation (Sec.~\ref{sec:cleaning})---if EEG and motion share $r{\approx}0.55$, isn't AAD just motion in disguise? The two findings together resolve it cleanly: \emph{a substantial fraction of EEG variance is motion- or arousal-correlated and is genuinely predictable from external sensors, but the attended-speaker-discriminating component lives in a smaller subspace that the cleaning ablation does not touch.} The next subsection isolates that subspace explicitly. The directional flow features (which-way did the camera pan) and the per-trial audio summaries are at zero predictability from EEG: EEG encodes \emph{motion amplitude}, not motion direction, and the AAD signal is not ``EEG looks like the spectrogram'' (which would require time-resolved TRF analysis on aligned series, not a per-trial summary).

\subsection{Trial-level motion-residualisation: a confound-controlled floor}
\label{sec:resid}

The strict control: force the trial-level EEG feature vector to be linearly independent of the trial-level motion feature vector. Per CV fold, we fit $M={\rm Ridge}(\alpha{=}10)$ on $Z_{\rm train} \to X_{\rm train}$ where $Z \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times 71}$ is the concatenated gaze (23-D) $+$ IMU (35-D) $+$ video (13-D) summary and $X \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times 368}$ is the EEG spectral feature matrix; we then replace $X$ on both train and test with their residuals $\tilde X = X - M(Z)$ and refit the AAD classifier. We compare to a \emph{shuffle-motion} null in which $Z$ is row-permuted across trials---a control that removes a 71-D subspace with no trial-level binding to $X$ and thus measures the capacity cost of orthogonalisation per se.

\begin{tabular}{llll}
\toprule
Task & raw & shuffle-motion & motion-residualised \\
\midrule
T1 hemisphere  & \textbf{0.716} [0.641, 0.793] & 0.643 [0.585, 0.701] ($p{=}10^{-3}$) & \textbf{0.521} [0.488, 0.552] ($p{=}6{\cdot}10^{-4}$) \\
T2 inner-outer & 0.650 [0.592, 0.714]           & 0.610 [0.559, 0.665] ($p{=}0.018$)    & 0.510 [0.480, 0.536] ($p{=}10^{-3}$) \\
T3 4-class     & 0.442 [0.357, 0.535]           & 0.392 [0.328, 0.462] ($p{=}0.017$)    & 0.270 [0.239, 0.296] ($p{=}4{\cdot}10^{-3}$) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

Orthogonalising with a 71-D \emph{shuffle} costs $7$\,pp on T1 (capacity cost of removing any 71-D subspace from a 368-D feature space). Orthogonalising with the \emph{real} motion features costs a further $12$\,pp---the motion-attributable component. The residualised number ($0.521$, lower CI $0.488$) sits at the chance floor: \textbf{a substantial fraction of the discriminative information in the 368-D classifier lives in a subspace that co-varies with head and gaze orientation}.

\paragraph{How to interpret this.} This is neither an artefact discovery nor a refutation of the AAD signal. Attending to a lateralised sound source produces both lateralised neural activity \emph{and} lateralised subtle head/gaze orientation; the two are aspects of the same attentional process and they share variance. What this control delivers is a confound-controlled floor ($0.521$, chance $0.5$) that future EEG-only or multimodal methods can target: a paper that claims a ``purely neural'' AAD gain on this dataset must move \emph{this} number, not the raw S1 number alone. Reconciling with Sec.~\ref{sec:cleaning}: time-domain channel-wise regression (which costs $-0.7$\,pp) and trial-level feature-space residualisation (which costs $-19.5$\,pp) answer different questions---the latter can remove signal that the former cannot reach because the classifier and the residualiser operate in the same trial-summary basis. A continuous sweep $X_\alpha = X - \alpha M(Z)$ for $\alpha \in [0,1]$ shows a smooth, monotone, approximately linear cost curve (Appendix~\ref{app:partial}), consistent with genuine entanglement rather than a clean separable subspace.

\subsection{Behaviour $\times$ decoder: comprehension vs.\ effort}
\label{sec:behxdec}

For every decoder we computed $\Delta_s = P(\text{decoder correct}\mid\text{behav.\ correct}) - P(\text{decoder correct}\mid\text{behav.\ wrong})$ per subject, then a signed Wilcoxon test across 16 subjects.

\begin{table}[ht]
\centering
\small
\caption{Within-subject behaviour-$\times$-decoder paired test. $\Delta_s$ is the per-subject difference in decoder accuracy between trials where the subject answered the comprehension question correctly vs.\ wrongly.}
\label{tab:behxdec}
\begin{tabular}{llrrr}
\toprule
Decoder & Task & Median $\Delta_s$ & Sign ($+/-$) & Wilcoxon $p$ \\
\midrule
Backward-TRF split $\delta+\theta$ & envelope-attendance & $-0.084$ & 3 / 13 & \textbf{0.034} \\
Backward-TRF broadband             & envelope-attendance & $-0.068$ & 5 / 11 & 0.093 \\

Two opposite-sign findings sit beside each other: (i)~gaze-based AAD is positively correlated with comprehension correctness ($p{=}0.044$); (ii)~envelope-tracking AAD is \emph{negatively} correlated with comprehension correctness, reaching nominal $p{<}0.05$ on the split $\delta+\theta$ TRF. The 368-D spectral classifier sits in between (median $\Delta_s{=}+0.037$, $p{=}0.18$) and effectively dissociates from both signs.

\paragraph{What this means for AAD methodology.} Gaze-based decoders mechanically track the comprehension-relevant orienting behaviour: subjects who comprehend more fixate the attended speaker more consistently. Envelope-tracking TRF decoders read out an acoustic cortico-speech alignment that the listener can sustain \emph{without} successfully recalling the question---and may in fact \emph{recruit more} on trials where comprehension is hard, consistent with effort/arousal accounts of low-frequency speech tracking. Two practical consequences for users of MAESTRO: (a)~a paper should not claim that a TRF-based AAD decoder reflects ``successful comprehension''---the trial-level data rule that out; (b)~the same dataset can therefore be used to develop \emph{listening-effort} probes (split $\delta+\theta$ TRFs as a candidate) that are statistically dissociable from comprehension-correlated gaze decoders.

The study was approved by the Institutional Review Board (protocol number and institution disclosed in the camera-ready version). Foreseeable misuses include using per-trial behavioural data to infer trait-level attributes of participants, and applying AAD decoders in surveillance contexts to infer listening targets; the CC-BY-NC-4.0 licence and access-by-request gate for the raw scene video are our mitigations.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limits}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Cohort size.} 16 subjects is adequate for within-subject AAD, matches the KUL corpus, and exceeds what is standard for new-paradigm AAD releases; it is however small for cross-subject (LOSO) generalisation studies and we report LOSO numbers as a calibration floor, not a deployment target.
  \item \textbf{Paradigm-specific.} The stereo-device design (one stereo file per device, two channels driving two speakers at different powers) makes within-device L/R discrimination near-impossible for envelope-reconstruction decoders and is what drives the chance-floor numbers in Sec.~\ref{sec:trf}. This is a feature for studying spatial attention in the multimodal regime (it decouples source identity from envelope content) but a limitation for porting classical two-talker AAD decoders unchanged.
  \item \textbf{One subject (S1) has hardware-bad mastoids.} Spherical-spline interpolation recovers a valid 32-ch trace, but S1 remains an outlier on several metrics. S1 is included in all pooled numbers; the high-quality-tier split S4 excludes S1 via a pre-registered gaze-validity criterion, never by post-hoc accuracy.
  \item \textbf{No deep-model ceilings in this release.} All headline numbers are linear or low-capacity. Deep EEG decoders---EEGNet, a 1-D CNN stimulus reconstructor, a LaBraM/BIOT foundation-model probe, and a cross-modal attention module---are implemented in \texttt{scripts/deep\_models.py} behind a \texttt{RUN\_DEEP} flag; running them is blocked on GPU allocation and will be added in a v1.1 analysis-pack update without changing any dataset bits.
  \item \textbf{Shared-variance confound.} The motion-residualisation control (Sec.~\ref{sec:resid}) shows that a substantial part of the spectral-classifier signal shares variance with head/gaze orientation. We interpret this as expected (shared cause: spatial attention drives both neural and orienting responses), but downstream work should prefer the motion-residualised floor when claiming purely neural gains.
  \item \textbf{Demographics partially opt-in.} Age and gender were collected only in a voluntary follow-up; we release only the aggregate range and do not stratify analyses by demographics.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

MAESTRO is a 16-subject, 1600-trial AAD dataset with five simultaneously recorded modalities, including head-mounted gaze, IMU, and scene video---modalities conspicuously missing from the existing AAD public record. Around the data we ship a versioned evaluation protocol with three task framings, four splits, six windows, and three mandatory baselines, plus a Python harness that enforces the contract. The benchmarks expose four findings that only a multimodal release can produce: (i)~classical envelope-reconstruction is at the chance floor of the paradigm and a 368-D linear spectral classifier is $+21.8$\,pp above it; (ii)~gaze alone outperforms either EEG decoder pooled, but per-subject calibrated late fusion never under-performs the stronger modality; (iii)~strict trial-level motion-residualisation collapses the spectral-classifier signal to chance, defining a confound-controlled floor that any ``purely neural'' decoder must beat; (iv)~at the trial level, gaze-based AAD tracks comprehension while envelope-tracking AAD anti-tracks it, dissociating effortful acoustic attention from delayed comprehension. The dataset gives the community the first AAD corpus on which the ``auditory attention or overt orientation'' question can be answered quantitatively on the same trials that produce the AAD number, and the first on which a new multimodal decoder can be scored against both a pooled accuracy target and a motion-orthogonal one.

\subsection{Storage layout and trial indexing}
\label{app:layout}

Per-subject, two directory trees hold parallel copies of the same trials partitioned by modality: \texttt{experiment\_data/Subject~N/} (EEG and audio playback timestamps) and \texttt{Video~Recordings/Subject~N/} (Tobii bundle: gaze, IMU, scene video, event data). Folders \texttt{1}--\texttt{5} are training trials, \texttt{6}--\texttt{105} are main trials. Subjects 1--2 use literal numeric folder names (\texttt{1}\ldots\texttt{105}). Subjects 3--16 use ISO-8601 timestamps (\texttt{YYYYMMDDTHHMMSSZ}); the loader parses the embedded time and sorts ascending to recover the natural recording order, verified empirically against audio playback timestamps to $r_\Delta \ge 0.99$ trial-to-trial for every subject (\texttt{results/01\_alignment\_check.parquet}).

\subsection{Cross-modal temporal alignment}
\label{app:align}

All streams are clipped to the shared audio-playback window $[t_0, t_1]$ recovered from each trial's \texttt{audio\_timestamps.json}. EEG's internal clock \code{eeg\_ts} is mapped to wall-clock time via the \code{first\_sample\_time} field in \texttt{eeg\_time\_data.p}. Tobii recording-relative timestamps are shifted to align their first sample to $t_0$. Audio envelopes are computed from each device's stimulus file and resampled to the target analysis rate (commonly \SI{64}{\Hz} to match the EEG low-pass). The alignment sanity check is shipped in \texttt{analysis/01\_data\_audit.ipynb}.

\subsection{Data quality audit (EEG)}
\label{app:eegquality}

\paragraph{Automatic bad-channel detection.} A channel is flagged bad if (i)~its standard deviation is below $10^{-9}$\,V (flat), (ii)~at least \SI{10}{\percent} of samples reach the \SI{0.0839}{\volt} ADC clip (saturation), or (iii)~its AC variance has $|z|>6$ relative to the median-absolute-deviation of the remaining channels. Per-subject and per-channel bad-rates are in \texttt{results/bad\_channels\_manifest.parquet} (Fig.~\ref{fig:eeg_quality}). The dominant failure mode is mastoid saturation: Subject~1 has 100\,\% flat M1 and 100\,\% saturated M2, and M2 saturation is common cohort-wide.

\paragraph{Robust referencing.} Linked mastoids when both M1/M2 are clean; common-average over the good channels otherwise; flagged channels then spherical-spline interpolated. Every trial therefore has 32 valid channels entering downstream analyses regardless of raw electrode issues.

\paragraph{Gaze tracking quality.} Tobii per-eye validity (the fraction of samples where the tracker locked a gaze vector) exceeds \SI{90}{\percent} for 14/16 subjects and is reported per-trial in \texttt{results/07\_gaze\_features.parquet}. Trials with $<\SI{50}{\percent}$ validity are flagged but not removed from the main release.

\subsection{Neuroscience validation}
\label{app:neuro}

The dataset reproduces canonical AAD neuroscience markers: (a)~FOOOF aperiodic slope at mid-parietal electrodes is $\approx 1.4$ (s.d.\ $<0.15$ across subjects), within the adult-awake range; (b)~theta--gamma phase-amplitude coupling peaks at $\theta\approx 6.5$\,Hz / $\gamma\approx 35$\,Hz (canonical); (c)~microstate k-means ($k=4$) recovers the canonical A/B/C/D morphologies with isotropic coverage; (d)~inter-subject correlation at Cz averages $r=0.023$ but peaks at $r=0.079$ on prosody climaxes (trial 7). Alpha lateralisation across the cohort is weak ($t=0.29$, $p=0.77$, $n=16$, 10-trial subset)---consistent with recent findings that the canonical alpha-lat AAD signature is paradigm-sensitive.
